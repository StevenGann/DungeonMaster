## Appearance

Lyra is a tall, athletic woman with sun-darkened skin and short-cropped black hair streaked with premature gray. A jagged scar runs from her left temple to her jaw—a souvenir from the Goblin Wars. She wears well-maintained chainmail and a blue cloak bearing the town's crest, and carries a longsword that's seen better days but is kept razor-sharp.

## Personality

**Traits:**
- Direct and no-nonsense
- Deeply protective of civilians
- Respects competence over rank
- Haunted by past failures

**Ideals:** The law exists to protect the innocent. Those who abuse power deserve no mercy.

**Bonds:** The soldiers under her command are her family.

**Flaws:** She refuses to ask for help, even when she needs it.

---

## Backstory

Lyra was a farm girl who enlisted after goblins raided her village and killed her parents. She fought in the Goblin Wars for eight years, rising through the ranks through sheer determination and tactical brilliance. When the war ended, she was offered a position in the king's army but chose instead to return to a quiet post where she could "do some good."

She's been Captain of the Millbrook Guard for five years. Under her leadership, bandit attacks have dropped significantly, though she's increasingly concerned about reports of monsters in the Greymist Hills.

---

## Motivations

- Protect Millbrook and its people
- Train competent replacements
- Find and destroy the goblin tribe that killed her family

---

## Secrets

- She still has nightmares about a battle where her squad was ambushed and she was the only survivor
- She's received reports of increased monster activity but can't get reinforcements from the capital
- Her second-in-command, Sergeant Bryce, is secretly taking bribes from smugglers

---

## Relationships

- **Sergeant Bryce:** Her second-in-command; she trusts him (mistakenly)
- **Lord Aldric:** The local noble; she despises him but follows orders
- **Marta Hearthstone:** Old friend; they play cards weekly
- **The Blacksmith's Son (Bram):** She's teaching him swordsmanship
